## Why it is not a safe detour (concrete reason)
The clearance (0.22-0.25 m) is the planner's inflation margin, smaller than the lateral tracking error measured on this robot (cross-track excursions 0.10-0.36 m on straight 3 m runs: E2E-02 0.18, P0 0.36),
and the detour needs a 0.65 m lateral shift in 1.6 m while D-05 makes realised curvature ~0.6 of the planned one. Indicative simulation (`trk.py`: pure pursuit, D-05 plant, heading drift calibrated so that the
max straight-line cross-track is 0.15 mean / 0.33 max): footprint-box contact in 1/300 runs (observed 0.20 m wide box) .. 6/300 (0.40 x 0.40 box) at the calibrated drift, 16/300 .. 37/300 at a pessimistic drift.
RPP's collision check (1.5 s on commanded speed = 0.45 m of arc, robot really covers 0.75 m, stop distance 0.2-0.3 m) would normally stop the robot before contact, which was not modelled, but a stop within a few
centimetres of the box is itself a dangerous approach. The simulation is indicative, not a measurement.

## Decision needed (nothing is changed without it)
(a) accept the nominal margin and rely on RPP's collision stop (risk: stop very close to the box, possible contact);
(b) increase the planner clearance (inflation radius / cost scaling): a planner/costmap change, explicitly outside the current allowance;
(c) first reduce the tracking error (D-05 closed-loop forward speed) — larger work.
Also needed: box dimensions (W x D x H) for a proper footprint check. Recommended: (b) if the operator allows a planner-side change, otherwise stay at stage 1.
